# Reference Directions

## Four References

### Meng Xie

Reference: https://www.mengxie.me/

- Content strategy: turns AI practice into a chronological journey instead of a list of tools.
- Strength: every claim is supported by a shipped project, number, workflow, or reflection.
- What to borrow: use real work to prove AI ability and connect experiments into one evolving point of view.
- What not to borrow directly: the very long timeline; our homepage should stay faster for recruiters to scan.

### Sander Chen

Reference: https://www.sanderchen.com/

- Content strategy: leads with positioning and evidence, then moves into a featured case and selected work.
- Strength: compact metrics, strong editorial hierarchy, and concise case-study language.
- What to borrow: evidence-first homepage, numbered projects, clear Problem / Approach / Outcome structure.
- What not to borrow directly: dense self-branding claims that are not yet supported by public projects.

### Isa de Burgh

Reference: https://isadeburgh.com/

- Content strategy: lets art direction and imagery create personality while keeping project metadata precise.
- Strength: expressive typography, intentional image rhythm, and a strong sense of the person behind the work.
- What to borrow: use portrait, travel, campus, and design artifacts as editorial punctuation rather than card decoration.
- What not to borrow directly: image-heavy project presentation before we have enough high-quality project assets.

### Sooah's Room Folio

Reference: https://sooahs-room-folio.com/

Source: https://github.com/andrewwoan/sooahs-room-folio

- Interaction strategy: a Three.js and Blender room turns personal objects into navigation and storytelling.
- Strength: the environment itself communicates personality; hovering and clicking reward exploration.
- What to borrow later: map a suitcase to travel, a sketchbook to industrial design, a phone or terminal to AI work, and a letter to campus projects.
- Constraint: keep the normal page underneath. Recruiters, mobile devices, reduced-motion users, and slower networks must still reach every important item without entering the 3D scene.

An additional simpler technical reference is Rowobin's open-source 3D room: https://github.com/Rowobin/3D-Room-Portfolio

## Combined Direction for WUTONG.WORLD

The site should combine four layers:

1. Sander's evidence-first clarity for the first screen and professional experience.
2. Meng's journey structure for AI learning and hands-on building.
3. Isa's editorial rhythm for portrait, personal history, travel, and visual artifacts.
4. Room Folio's object-based interaction as a later optional exploration mode.

V1 should remain a fast editorial portfolio. The 3D room becomes a signature second entrance after the content and assets are mature.
